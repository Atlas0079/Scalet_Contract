# Armed Taoists and Corpse Animation (Draft)

This concept is proposed as a second-tier supporting setting: armed Taoists combine Taoist religious imagery with bioengineering to animate dead bodies on the battlefield. Sects, characters, and organizational histories are third-tier extensions and remain undeveloped. The main proposed features follow; technical explanations and detailed rules remain open.

## Tactical role

A Taoist enters combat with a few corpses maintained for repeated use, then temporarily expands the group with fallen enemies or allies. The operator retains control of their own body while commanding corpses. Temporary corpses have short operating lives, so the resulting local advantage must be used promptly.

Animation requires reaching the corpse and physically treating it. A peachwood sword passes through the head at the temples and remains implanted throughout control. The hilt and tip protrude on opposite sides of the head, forming a distinctive silhouette with talismans near the face. Talismans and control devices support commands for movement, simple shooting, and cover; bodies can also be used to obstruct attacks.

Fresh corpses operate longer. Decayed corpses require additional nanomachine fluid and synthetic blood, yet remain active only briefly. Physical damage constrains available actions; a corpse able to stand may still be unable to hold and fire a gun.

## Maintained and temporary corpses

| Aspect | Maintained corpses | Temporary corpses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preparation | Cultivation, modification, and maintenance at base | Rapid physical treatment in the field |
| Purpose | Repeated deployment and a stable core force | Short tasks within a limited operating life |
| Equipment | Weapons and supplies fitted in advance | Existing gear or weapons issued on site |
| Main costs | Preparation and ongoing upkeep | Implants, talismans, fluids, and treatment time |

The operator carries spare swords, talismans, cheap guns, ammunition, and working fluids. Exposure while reaching bodies, treatment time, carrying capacity, and corpse condition constrain expansion. Converting temporary corpses into maintained ones remains undecided.

## Technical direction

The preferred explanation uses bioengineering and control technology, without requiring psionics. The implanted sword could house a control node, and talismans could carry programs or modes. Nanomachines connect surviving tissue, while synthetic blood and an artificial circulation system support limited activity. Materials, talisman construction, and repair limits remain proposals.

Corpses receive simple commands executed by local controllers. The operator lacks SIA-style precise body control over extreme distances. Corpse animation may share maintenance technology and consumables with androids; body classification and control rules require separate definition.

The proposal does not require restoring the deceased person’s consciousness, nor does animation restore all previous skills. Residual memories, skill sources, and consciousness in maintained corpses remain open.

## Rules to connect

- **Control and SIA:** Define actions the local controller can execute, judgments requiring the operator, and the distinction from pilot-controlled robots.
- **Commands and termination:** Determine medium, range, obstruction, link loss, operator incapacity, and the effects of removing or damaging the sword.
- **Bodies and death:** Determine eligible bodies, required tissue integrity, shooting skill sources, and skill ownership after death.
- **Forces and resources:** Determine control capacity, treatment time, operating life, fluid replenishment, and device recovery.
- **Weapons and protection:** Determine gun handling, reloading, and equipment use; bodies obstructing attacks must fit existing hit and penetration rules.
